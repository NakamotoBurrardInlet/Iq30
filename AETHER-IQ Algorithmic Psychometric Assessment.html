Core Concepts of Intelligence & Algorithmic Psychometrics

1. Spearman’s g-Factor

Discovered via factor analysis by Charles Spearman (1904), the positive manifold reveals that individuals who excel in one cognitive domain tend to excel across all others. g represents the shared variance across diverse mental operations—the underlying computational bandwidth of the cortex.

2. Fluid (Gf) vs. Crystallized (Gc)

Under the Cattell-Horn-Carroll (CHC) taxonomy, Gf governs novel induction, working memory manipulation, and abstract rule discovery, whereas Gc reflects the depth, precision, and structural organization of acquired conceptual knowledge.

3. Kolmogorov Complexity & Compression

In algorithmic information theory, intelligence is formalized as the capacity to discover the shortest generative program K(x) that predicts an observed sequence. High-IQ pattern recognition is mathematically equivalent to lossless inductive compression.

Foundations of Psychometrics & Algorithmic Intelligence

01 Spearman’s General Factor (g) & Factor Extraction

When any sufficiently broad battery of cognitive tasks is administered to a population, every pair of tests correlates positively—a phenomenon known as the positive manifold. Applying principal axis factoring to the correlation matrix extracts a dominant first unrotated factor, denoted g, which typically accounts for 40% to 55% of total inter-individual variance.

Tasks with high g-loading—such as deductive syllogisms, number series induction, and abstract matrix completion—require active manipulation of relational representations rather than rote recall.

02 Cattell–Horn–Carroll (CHC) Stratum II Architecture

Modern psychometrics organizes intelligence hierarchically into three strata. Beneath the apex (g) reside broad cognitive abilities:

  • Fluid Reasoning (Gf): Induction, deduction, and quantitative reasoning in unfamiliar domains.
  • Crystallized Intelligence (Gc): Lexical precision, conceptual taxonomy, and semantic analogical mapping.
  • Working Memory Capacity (Gwm): Maintaining and transforming multi-step intermediate states without interference.
  • Processing Speed (Gs): Rapid, accurate execution of elementary cognitive operations.

03 Algorithmic Information Theory & Solomonoff Induction

From a computational perspective, intelligence is tightly bound to Kolmogorov Complexity K(s)—the length of the shortest binary program that outputs string s on a universal Turing machine.

When solving sequence or matrix problems, the human mind performs approximate Solomonoff Induction: weighing candidate hypotheses inversely by their descriptive length (2⁻ˡ) to identify the simplest generative law governing apparent complexity.

04 Gaussian Deviation IQ & Multi-Radix Representation

Classical ratio IQ (MA/CA × 100) was superseded by David Wechsler’s Deviation IQ, which defines scores strictly by their position on a standard normal distribution with mean μ = 100 and standard deviation σ = 15.

In AETHER-IQ, your standardized integer score is simultaneously projected into Base-2 (Binary) and Base-16 (Hexadecimal) machine registers to illustrate how cognitive metrics map across symbolic number systems.
